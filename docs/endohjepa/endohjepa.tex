\begin{abstract}
Minimally invasive diagnosis and therapy are increasingly performed through
endoscopes, where safety depends on anticipating how the scene will evolve;
yet endoscopic AI remains reactive, recognising the present frame without
forecasting the next. Two problems follow. First, existing surgical video
models are built per orifice and at a single temporal scale, although
laparoscopy, gastrointestinal endoscopy and bronchoscopy share the same
physics of a camera advancing through a deformable lumen. Second, world-model
research assumes, without testing, that predictive video features are
physically grounded enough to score or plan camera motion; we show this
assumption fails unless the action path is explicitly measured and audited.
We present Endo-HJEPA, a hierarchical joint-embedding world model that
predicts future \emph{representations}, not pixels, from a frozen V-JEPA~2
ViT-L encoder shared across the three orifices. A causal residual Transformer
forecasts short-horizon dynamics, a coarse predictor covers mid-horizon
anatomy, and a block-causal branch conditions on measured continuous SE(3)
camera increments with probabilistic uncertainty, calibrated risk and a hard
safety gate. Four technical contributions support the grounding claim: a
block-causal action-attention path with executable input-sensitivity tests, a
metric SE(3) camera-motion interface with fractional tubelet alignment,
continuous planning constrained to the normalised action support with hard
rejection, and a reprojection gate that verifies pose conventions against
ground-truth depth before training. The design is validated on a leakage-free video-level protocol
over 19 datasets and 1,707 sequences: the causal forecaster reaches 0.978
cosine at horizon four versus 0.916 for persistence, 0.974 for a GRU and 0.971
for Mamba (Holm-corrected Wilcoxon $p<10^{-80}$), improves from 500 to 6,000
training clips, and plateaus at 13,552. For physical grounding, an initial
action branch failed its prespecified sensitivity gate (73.8\% versus 80\%);
a correctness audit traced the failure to stereo-mixed inputs, non-causal
encoding, chunk boundaries and negative-sampling mismatch, and the corrected
pipeline passes the gate on held-out SCARED cases (83.1\%; grouped
cross-validation 88.9\%; three seeds 85.9--86.5\%). Behaviour-constrained
continuous model-predictive control then passes all offline navigation gates
(200 trials, 51.5\% reach, 29.0\% pose-error reduction versus persistence).
External C3VD trajectories remain above chance but below gate (pooled 58.3\%,
$n=798$), and calibrated near-wall risk does not generalise across cases
(AUC $\le0.54$); we report both as characterised limits. The three validated
capabilities (forecasting scene evolution, evaluating the visual consequence
of a measured camera motion, and model-based navigation planning) map
directly onto loss-of-view warning, camera-handling training, and
navigation-assistance prototypes. Robot-executed control and clinical
collision warning remain outside the validated boundary. Endo-HJEPA thus
delivers a reproducible cross-orifice predictive foundation and an audited
geometry-grounding protocol, while defining precisely where physically
grounded control does and does not follow from passive video features.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Clinical context: endoscopy needs anticipation, not only recognition}
Minimally invasive diagnosis and therapy are increasingly delivered through an endoscope: laparoscopes in the abdomen, flexible scopes in the gastrointestinal tract, and bronchoscopes in the airway.
Across all three orifices, safe operation depends on a clinician's ability to \emph{anticipate}: how tissue will deform under contact, how a lumen will open as the scope advances, where an instrument will move, and when the camera is about to collide with a wall or lose the view.
Current endoscopic AI is almost entirely \emph{reactive}.
It recognises the present frame (surgical phase, instrument presence, anatomy) but cannot forecast what comes next, nor what a candidate camera adjustment would produce.
A model that predicts how an endoscopic scene will evolve is a prerequisite for
anticipatory guidance and collision warning. Querying counterfactual camera
motion additionally requires metric action supervision; predictive video
features alone are insufficient. That distinction, forecast first and then
physical grounding under explicit SE(3) and depth signals, is the clinical and
technical target of this paper.
Three application pathways motivate the design: \emph{loss-of-view warning}
(predict how the view will evolve before it is lost), \emph{camera-handling
training and assessment} (evaluate what a candidate camera motion would show,
without executing it), and \emph{navigation assistance} (plan camera motion
toward a target view). Each pathway corresponds to one capability we validate
or refute.

\subsection{The technical problem: structure is predictable, appearance is not}
A world model for endoscopy faces a specific asymmetry.
The \emph{structure} of endoscopic video (lumen geometry, camera advance, instrument trajectories) follows smooth physical dynamics and is strongly predictable.
The \emph{appearance} (specular highlights, fluid, smoke, mucosal micro-texture, peristalsis) is high-entropy and largely unpredictable.
The dominant world-model paradigm, pixel generation (diffusion world-action models, Genie-style token video), must spend capacity forecasting this unpredictable appearance.
That both wastes capacity and ties the model to a pixel metric that is dominated by glare and fluid rather than by the quantities that matter for planning.
Joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPA) offer the alternative we adopt: predict in a learned representation space, where unpredictable appearance residual can be ignored by design, and where the predicted quantities are exactly the plannable ones.

\paragraph{Key insight: predict plannable representations, not the next frame.}
If a frame is written as structure plus appearance, any pixel predictor is lower-bounded by the appearance dispersion, regardless of how well structure is modelled (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).
A representation that is sufficient for structure and invariant to appearance has no such bound.
Endoscopy is an unusually clean instance of this split: the camera moves through a deformable lumen under clinician control (predictable), while highlights and fluid dominate the pixel residual (unpredictable).
The design consequence is that a world model should (i)~forecast in encoder space, (ii)~separate fast dynamics from slow anatomy, (iii)~score which futures are plausible, and (iv)~plan by rolling those futures, without ever decoding pixels.

\subsection{Gap: three missing pieces and one untested assumption}
Despite rapid progress in surgical video understanding, three gaps remain.
\textbf{(i) Single scale.}
Existing surgical JEPA and video-pretraining systems (SurgRec-JEPA, JHU-VPT, VideoMAE) learn a single temporal predictor and do not separate fast tissue/tool/camera dynamics from slow anatomical and procedural structure.
LeCun's hierarchical JEPA (H-JEPA) argues such separation is essential for long-horizon prediction and planning, but it has not been instantiated for endoscopy.
\textbf{(ii) Per-orifice fragmentation.}
Endoscopic models are built per orifice: laparoscopy, GI endoscopy, and bronchoscopy are treated as separate foundation-model problems, even though all three share the same underlying physics of a camera advancing through a deformable lumen under clinician control.
A single domain-conditioned world model is both a stronger scientific claim and a more data-efficient one, but it requires evidence that such sharing is beneficial, or necessary.
\textbf{(iii) Pixel-generation mismatch.}
World models tailored to surgery are predominantly pixel-generative, spending
capacity on the appearance residual that endoscopy makes unpredictable.
\textbf{(iv) The untested grounding assumption: the new problem we target.}
Recent endoscopic world models report latent reach or energy scores and imply
physical utility, but none verifies that the predicted quantities are actually
sensitive to measured camera motion, nor that their evaluation code computes
what it claims. We show that both failure modes occur in practice: an
unexecuted encoder can produce perfect-looking planning scores, and a
state-of-the-art video encoder can pass a forecast benchmark while its history
tokens carry future content. Physical grounding must be measured against
metric SE(3) motion and depth, under leakage-free splits, with correctness
audits, not inferred from forecast accuracy.

\subsection{Our approach}
We instantiate this idea in \textbf{Endo-HJEPA} (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}).
A shared frozen V-JEPA~2 ViT-L encoder maps clips from all three orifices to dense tokens.
Two validated domain-conditioned predictors operate at increasing temporal
abstraction: L1 is causal, residual and short-horizon; L2 is temporally pooled.
For physical grounding, a separate block-causal branch consumes continuous
camera-frame SE(3) increments aligned to SCARED/C3VD tubelets. A factorised
adapter exposes geometry, tool and slow semantic state to the planner while
isolating nuisance variation. Probabilistic ensemble dynamics, calibrated
near-wall risk and a hard safety gate define the prospective continuous
CEM/MPPI interface.
The encoder is frozen by default so that every dynamics ablation shares identical latents; an optional last-block adaptation is evaluated only on recognition probes.
No decoder is trained.
The evidence is scoped as follows: forecast and scale are measured on a
6{,}000-clip causal-L1 protocol; the physical branch is trained and selected
under a case-level split with grouped cross-validation, and reported on a
frozen test case; recognition uses frozen versus adapted V-JEPA~2 on the
official CholecT50 split.
These protocols are never mixed in a single table.

\subsection{Contributions and significance}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A hierarchical JEPA predictive foundation for unified cross-orifice endoscopy.}
  We instantiate short-horizon residual dynamics and mid-horizon context on the official V-JEPA~2 ViT-L encoder across laparoscopy, GI endoscopy, and bronchoscopy with one domain-conditioned model.
  \item \textbf{A causal-autoregressive latent forecaster.}
  The \emph{form} of the latent forecaster matters more than its size: a GPT-style causal Transformer surpasses a strong GRU and a Mamba/SSM baseline, whereas a parallel query-token Transformer of the same capacity does not.
  \item \textbf{An audited path from prediction to physical safety.}
  We show that unsupervised latent actions are not control variables, exclude the invalid discrete-MPC evidence, and build a corrected continuous SE(3) branch that passes its prespecified action-sensitivity gate on held-out SCARED cases (83.1\%) after four audit-identified data defects are fixed; behaviour-constrained continuous CEM then passes all offline navigation gates (200 trials, 51.5\% reach). The audit itself is a method contribution: executable input-sensitivity tests, a depth-reprojection pose gate, matched counterfactual negative banks, and grouped cross-validation with a frozen test set (Section~\ref{sec:audit}). External C3VD generalisation and calibrated near-wall risk remain below gate and are reported as characterised limits.
  \item \textbf{A leakage-free, per-dataset protocol.}
  Video-level splits, domain-balanced sampling, per-dataset forecast decomposition, reproducible external baselines, grouped cross-validation with a frozen test set, and paired Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction, released as executable code.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Findings preview.}
On 19 datasets and 1{,}707 sequences the causal forecaster reaches 0.978
cosine at horizon four against 0.916 persistence, 0.974 GRU and 0.971 Mamba
(Holm-corrected $p<10^{-80}$), with monotone data-scaling to 6{,}000 clips and
a plateau at 13{,}552. Two characterised negative results complete the
picture: self-supervised latent actions are not semantically or physically
grounded without encoder-level supervision, and zero-shot cross-orifice
transfer fails but is recoverable with a 32-shot domain token, evidence that
unified multi-orifice training is necessary, not merely convenient.
We do \emph{not} claim the first surgical world model, pixel-level video
generation, robot-executed planning, uniform recognition SOTA, or any clinical
efficacy endpoint.

\paragraph{Significance.}
Endo-HJEPA reframes endoscopic AI from reactive recognition to anticipatory world modelling.
Three properties matter clinically: anticipation (forecast is the primitive
behind collision warning), calibrated physical risk rather than latent
confidence, and unification across orifices where labelled video is scarce.
The current real-data evidence establishes the first and provides only a weak
pilot for the second.

\section{Related work}
We position Endo-HJEPA along five axes: the world-model lineage it builds on, the surgical/endoscopic representation learning it extends, the surgical world models it differs from, the general video/image backbones we benchmark against, and the endoscopic datasets that anchor our evaluation.

\subsection{World models and joint-embedding prediction}
The idea of a learned world model for prediction and planning originates with Ha and Schmidhuber's World Models~\cite{ha2018worldmodels} and the PlaNet latent-dynamics planner~\cite{hafner2019planet}, and was scaled to diverse control domains by Dreamer~\cite{hafner2023dreamer}. These models predict a learned latent state and plan by rolling it forward, but they are trained with a reconstruction or reward signal and target control, not video understanding. Energy-based formulations~\cite{lecun2006ebm} provide the formal basis for scoring state compatibility without a generative decoder. The joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) of LeCun's position paper~\cite{lecun2022path} argues that predicting in representation space, rather than pixels, is the key to capturing predictable structure while ignoring unpredictable appearance; I-JEPA~\cite{assran2023ijepa} realised this for images, V-JEPA~\cite{bardes2024vjepa} for video, and V-JEPA~2~\cite{assran2025vjepa2} added scale and the action-conditioned (V-JEPA 2-AC) planning variant. Generative interactive environments such as Genie~\cite{genie2024} take the opposite, pixel-generative route. A common limitation of these works, for our purpose, is that they are either single-scale (no temporal hierarchy), pixel-generative (capacity spent on unpredictable appearance), or evaluated on generic/control video rather than endoscopic dynamics. Endo-HJEPA adopts the JEPA principle and adds the hierarchy and energy prior that the surgical setting lacks.

\subsection{Self-supervised surgical and endoscopic video representation}
Self-supervised pretraining has become the default for surgical video. SurgRec-JEPA~\cite{surgrecjepa} and JHU-VPT~\cite{jhuvpt} apply JEPA-style and video pretraining to surgical data and report strong downstream transfer, and endoscopic foundation models such as Endo-FM~\cite{endofoundation} scale self-supervised pretraining to large endoscopic corpora. These methods produce a single-scale representation for \emph{recognition} (phase, tools, segmentation); none of them build a dynamics model that can forecast or plan. Endo-HJEPA shares their self-supervised, frozen-encoder efficiency but adds a temporal hierarchy and an explicitly supervised geometry-action interface.

\subsection{Surgical and endoscopic world models}
World models tailored to surgery are emerging. The Surgical Vision World Model~\cite{svwm} generates pixel-controllable surgical video; SurgWorld~\cite{surgworld} learns latent actions for cataract surgery; EndoWAM~\cite{endowam} applies diffusion world-action modelling to endoscopic navigation; and Surgical WAM~\cite{surgicalwam} couples generative video with robot action chunks for closed-loop control. These are predominantly \emph{pixel-generative} or \emph{single-task}. Our contribution is orthogonal and complementary: hierarchical representation prediction together with an audit showing that metric action, calibrated uncertainty and hard safety constraints cannot be inferred from passive JEPA features alone.

\subsection{General video and image representation backbones}
For the representation evaluation we benchmark against the strongest \emph{reproducible} general-purpose encoders. For video: VideoMAE~\cite{tong2022videomae} (Kinetics masked autoencoding), TimeSformer~\cite{timesformer} (divided space-time attention), and ViViT~\cite{vivit} (factorised space-time); state-space video models such as VideoMamba~\cite{videomamba} and the underlying Mamba block~\cite{mamba} are the recurrent/SSM reference for dynamics. For images: the ImageNet-supervised ViT~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} and DINOv2 self-supervised features~\cite{dinov2}, pooled over time. None of these provide a world-model predictor or a planning/energy capability, which is the gap Endo-HJEPA fills; \S\ref{sec:results} measures them on the same CholecT50 protocol.

\subsection{Endoscopic datasets and physical supervision signals}
Our evaluation spans the standard endoscopic corpora: Cholec80 (phase + tool presence)~\cite{twinanda2017endonet}, CholecT50 (action triplets + phase)~\cite{nwoye2022rendezvous,nwoye2022cholectsplit}, EndoVis 2017/2018 (instrument segmentation)~\cite{endovis2017}, CholecSeg8k (semantic segmentation)~\cite{hong2020cholecseg8k}, endoscapes (segmentation + critical view of safety)~\cite{endoscapes}, the GI corpora HyperKvasir~\cite{borgli2020hyperkvasir} and Kvasir-Capsule~\cite{kvasircapsule}, and the pose/depth corpora SCARED~\cite{allan2021scared} and C3VD~\cite{bobrow2023c3vd}, with STIR~\cite{stir2024} for deformable point tracks. To our knowledge, using SCARED/C3VD camera poses and STIR point tracks as \emph{physical anchors} for latent actions, energy, and deformation, rather than as reconstruction targets, is a novel use of these supervision signals within a JEPA world model.

\paragraph{Summary.} Across the five axes, the field offers strong single-scale SSL representations, pixel-generative surgical world models, and general video/image backbones, but no hierarchical, energy-based JEPA world model trained and evaluated as a unified cross-orifice endoscopic system with a physically grounded uncertainty signal. That is the gap Endo-HJEPA occupies.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Core idea.}
Endo-HJEPA separates two queries without generating pixels:
(i)~given a history of endoscopic tokens, what is the likely future
representation; and (ii)~given a \emph{measured camera motion}, what is the
distribution of physically plausible futures and their near-wall risk.
L1/L2 answer the validated forecast query. The second query is deliberately
conditioned on continuous SE(3), not on an emergent residual code.
The two queries support the three capabilities evaluated in
Section~\ref{sec:results}: forecasting scene evolution (query i),
action-conditioned consequence evaluation (query ii), and model-based
navigation planning (query ii rolled forward under CEM).
Residual anchoring and uncertainty weighting belong to the core pooled model.
Dense VICReg, appearance weighting and STIR supervision are explicitly
separated below as optional spatial or encoder-adaptation extensions; they are
not silently mixed into the pooled forecast numbers.
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} gives the study-level data flow,
Figure~\ref{fig:network} gives the tensor-level model, and
Table~\ref{tab:notation} lists symbols.

\subsection{Problem formulation and assumptions}
\label{sec:formulation}
A clip $x_{1:T}$ from orifice $d$ is mapped by a frozen encoder $f_\theta$ to
\begin{equation}
z_{1:T'}=f_\theta(x_{1:T})\in\mathbb{R}^{T'\times N\times D},\qquad T'=T/2,\; N=256,\; D=1024.
\label{eq:encode}
\end{equation}
Headline pooled forecast operates on the spatially pooled token
\begin{equation}
\bar z_t=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}z_{t,n}\in\mathbb{R}^{D},
\label{eq:pool}
\end{equation}
averaging over the $N$ spatial sites. The world model is a collection of
predictors $g_\phi$ on $\bar z_t$ (pooled) or $z_{t,n}$ (dense extension) and
never decodes pixels. The passive branch has two validated temporal scales:
L1 forecasts $\hat z_{t+1:t+H}$ (tissue, tool, camera), whereas L2 forecasts a
stride-$2$ pooled stream (anatomy / phase). For physical grounding, a low-rank
adapter decomposes $z_t\mapsto(s_t^g,s_t^q,s_t^m,\xi_t)$ for downstream
slot-level analysis and loss construction. The implemented planner rolls the
full pooled latent $\bar z_t$ directly (the factorised adapter is not in the
CEM/MPPI loop), so the continuous MPC objective is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat u_{t:t+H-1}
={}&\arg\min_{u_{t:t+H-1}}\;
\frac{1}{D}\bigl\|\hat{\bar z}_{t+H}(u_{t:t+H-1})-\bar z^\star\bigr\|_2^2
+\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}
\bigl[
\lambda_r p_{\mathrm{wall},t+k}\\
&{}+\lambda_u\,
(\overline{\operatorname{Var}}_{\mathrm{ale},t+k}
+\overline{\operatorname{Var}}_{\mathrm{epi},t+k})
\bigr]
+\lambda_s\,\frac{1}{6(H-1)}\sum_{k=1}^{H-1}
\|u_{t+k}-u_{t+k-1}\|_2^2
+\lambda_e\,\overline{E}_{\mathrm{path}} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mpc}
\end{equation}
Here $\overline{\operatorname{Var}}$ denotes the mean over the $D$ latent
dimensions (not the trace), and $\overline{E}_{\mathrm{path}}$ is the mean
per-step prediction energy along the rollout; $\lambda_e$ is the optional
energy weight ($0$ when the legacy discrete head is not used). We deliberately
match the implementation, which averages rather than sums variances over
dimensions.
Candidates above collision-risk or uncertainty thresholds are rejected
independently of the soft cost.

\emph{Assumptions.}
(i)~Endoscopic dynamics are approximately Markovian in the learned latent space at the tubelet timescale.
(ii)~The frozen V-JEPA~2 encoder is more predictable for structure than for appearance (tested by the pixel-generation contrast in \S\ref{sec:rq1}).
(iii)~Orifice domains share enough low-level dynamics that a domain-conditioned predictor can serve all three, while differing enough that naive zero-shot transfer fails (tested in \S\ref{sec:rq4}).

\subsection{Shared encoder and optional endoscopic token weighting}
We use \texttt{facebook/vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256} (ViT-L, patch $16$, tubelet $2$, input $256^2$), frozen by default.
An optional flag unfreezes the last $K$ blocks for recognition-oriented domain adaptation; that path is never mixed into the forecast tables.
High-luminance, low-saturation tubelets (glare, fluid highlights) are weakly predictable and are down-weighted to a floor $w_{\min}{=}0.25$.
When EndoVis instrument masks are available, instrument tubelets are up-weighted by $1+\beta\, m_{\mathrm{inst}}$.
This mask-weighted loss is an optional scratch-JEPA/encoder-adaptation path,
used for the mask analysis rather than the official frozen-V-JEPA~2 pooled
forecast protocol. The main 6,000-clip and 13,552-clip forecast models
therefore use uniform latent weights. Making that distinction avoids
attributing their gains to supervision that they did not receive.

\subsection{Hierarchical predictors}
\label{sec:predictors}
All predictors are Transformer-based.
A shared domain embedding $e_d$ is added to the history so that one set of weights serves three orifices.

\paragraph{L1, causal autoregressive (default pooled forecaster).}
A GPT-style causal Transformer predicts the next pooled token from the causal context and feeds the prediction back for $H$ steps.
Let $h_t=\mathrm{in}(z_t)+e_d+p_t$ be the projected history with positional encoding $p_t$, and let $M$ be the upper-triangular causal mask.
One rollout step is
\begin{equation}
\hat z_{t+1}
=
z_t
+
\mathrm{head}\bigl(
\mathrm{Transformer}(h_{\le t};M)
\bigr)_{t},
\label{eq:causal}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the network predicts a \emph{residual} from the last observed (or last predicted) token.
This matches the near-Markov, smooth structure of endoscopic camera motion.
Section~\ref{sec:rq1} shows that this inductive bias, not parameter count, is what lets the Transformer surpass a GRU: a parallel query-token Transformer of the same size does not.

\paragraph{L1, dense spatio-temporal.}
For spatially resolved prediction a factorised block applies temporal attention per spatial site, then spatial attention per tubelet step, over all $N{=}256$ tokens.
Learned positional embeddings are added along both axes; $H$ query steps are appended along time.
This captures global camera motion that independent per-site prediction cannot represent.
In the optional dense configuration, dense L1 is trained jointly with the
pooled head so that its pooled evaluation path is not an untrained leftover.
The principal scale and recurrent-baseline experiments use the pooled causal
configuration; dense prediction is a spatial extension, not the source of the
reported 0.978 pooled score.

\paragraph{Residual (delta) prediction.}
Consecutive endoscopic latents are highly correlated.
Every predictor therefore forecasts the change from the last token of the
current sequence, which is the observed token at step 1 and the previously
predicted token thereafter. In pooled space:
\begin{equation}
\hat z_{t+1}=\bar z_t+g_{\phi,1}(\bar z_{t-H_{\mathrm{hist}}+1:t},e_d),
\qquad
\hat z_{t+k}=\hat z_{t+k-1}+g_{\phi,k}(\cdot),
\qquad k=2,\ldots,H .
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
The anchor is the running last token, not a fixed clip-final index.
The parameterisation contains persistence as the zero-residual solution but
does not guarantee improvement; without residual anchoring a $70$M predictor
is high-variance and can fall below persistence
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{L2, coarse mid-horizon.}
History tokens are average-pooled along time with stride 2, i.e.
$\bar z^{\mathrm{hist}}_t\leftarrow\mathrm{AvgPool}_2(\bar z^{\mathrm{hist}}_t)$.
The future targets are subsampled at the same stride
($\bar z^{\mathrm{fut}}_{2}, \bar z^{\mathrm{fut}}_{4}, \ldots$) and the
predictor is trained to forecast this coarser stream from the pooled history.
L2 adds little to short-horizon pooled cosine; its role is a seconds-scale handle for anatomy and phase.

\paragraph{Block-causal continuous action branch.}
SCARED/C3VD camera-to-world poses are interpolated on SE(3) to fractional
encoder-tubelet centres before differencing. The local action is
$u_t=\log(\mathbf{T}_t^{-1}\mathbf{T}_{t+1})^\vee=[v_t;\omega_t]$. Observed history tokens attend
bidirectionally within the history block; future query $k$ attends to all
history and action queries $1{:}k$, never to later actions. Changing history,
domain or action must change the prediction, and gradients must reach both the
action projection and Transformer; these properties are executable unit tests.
The legacy $K{=}16$ residual codebook is retained only as a negative grounding
baseline (Section~\ref{sec:rq5}).

\paragraph{Energy and calibrated risk.}
$E(z,a,z')$ is an MLP on $[z;\mathrm{emb}(a);z']$, where
$a\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}$ is a \emph{discrete} residual code (distinct from the
continuous SE(3) twist $u_t$). It is trained contrastively with a rolled
negative $z^{-}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_E
=
\mathrm{softplus}\bigl(
E(z_t,a_t,z_{t+1})-E(z_t,a_t,z^{-})
\bigr).
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
Low energy marks in-distribution, predictable futures, but energy is not
labelled as collision probability. The grounded branch instead predicts
heteroscedastic variance, uses ensemble disagreement for epistemic uncertainty,
and trains a near-wall head from SCARED depth. Temperature scaling and
split-conformal intervals are fitted only on a held-out calibration split.
For ensemble member $m$, diagonal Gaussian dynamics minimise
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NLL}}^{(m)}
=
\frac{1}{2BHD_s}
\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{k=1}^{H}\sum_{j=1}^{D_s}
\left[
\log \sigma_{m,bkj}^{2}
+
\frac{(s_{bkj}^{\mathrm{plan}}-\mu_{m,bkj})^2}
{\sigma_{m,bkj}^{2}}
\right],
\label{eq:nll}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is batch size and $j$ indexes planner-state dimensions. The reported
diagonal uncertainties are
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{ale}}=\operatorname{Diag}
(M_e^{-1}\sum_m\sigma_m^2)$ and
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{epi}}=\operatorname{Diag}
(M_e^{-1}\sum_m(\mu_m-\bar\mu)^2)$, with
$\bar\mu=M_e^{-1}\sum_m\mu_m$.

\paragraph{Continuous MPC and safety gate.}
CEM or MPPI optimises bounded six-dimensional actions under~\eqref{eq:mpc}.
Candidates are sampled in the model's \emph{normalised} action space (zero
mean, unit variance per axis), so proposals stay within the training support;
raw-space sampling would place rotation candidates 100--380 standard
deviations out of distribution. The hard gate replaces every rejected sequence
by a zero-motion fallback.
Evaluation is receding-horizon and offline; no candidate is described as
clinically executable without device telemetry and prospective validation.

\subsection{Correctness auditing and evaluation gates}
\label{sec:audit}
World-model claims are only as strong as the code that computes them. We
therefore treat verification as part of the method, not as post-hoc
debugging. Four executable gates accompany the model.
\paragraph{(i) Input-sensitivity unit tests.}
Every action-conditioned predictor must pass executable tests before its
metrics are read: perturbing history, domain or candidate action must change
the output; future actions must not affect earlier predictions; gradients must
reach the action projection and the Transformer encoder. These tests caught a
fatal defect in which the L2/L3 encoder was constructed but never executed
(Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).
\paragraph{(ii) Reprojection-gated pose conventions.}
Before any pose-conditioned training, candidate camera-frame conventions are
verified by reprojecting ground-truth depth between frames under the dataset's
official intrinsics; the convention must achieve sub-pixel median error. This
gate revealed that C3VD poses require a transpose plus an OpenGL$\to$OpenCV
axis flip (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}).
\paragraph{(iii) Counterfactual negative matching.}
Action sensitivity is measured by whether the real action beats a
\emph{matched} wrong action. Training and evaluation use the same negative
distribution (same-sequence, temporally adjacent hard negatives), and
evaluation uses a fixed negative bank so the metric does not depend on batch
order.
\paragraph{(iv) Grouped development with a frozen test set.}
Model and loss choices are made by leave-one-case-out cross-validation over
training cases; the test case is evaluated at most once per locked variant,
and the audit history of every test-set contact is recorded.

\subsection{Objective}
\label{sec:objective}
Each active passive-prediction loss $\mathcal{L}_i$ carries a learnable
log-variance $s_i$ (multi-task weighting), contributing
$e^{-s_i}\mathcal{L}_i+s_i$. This quantity is not predictive uncertainty. The
validated pooled objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pool}}
=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{pool}}}
\bigl(e^{-s_i}\mathcal{L}_i+s_i\bigr).
\label{eq:obj}
\end{equation}
The causal-L1 scale experiment uses
$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{pool}}=\{\mathrm{L1}\}$; the two-level pooled hierarchy
uses $\{\mathrm{L1},\mathrm{L2}\}$.
The grounded branch adds Gaussian rollout likelihood, forward--inverse action
cycle, SE(3), depth, tool-mask, slot-separation, frozen-teacher fidelity and
near-wall classification terms. Its predictive uncertainty is the
heteroscedastic covariance plus ensemble disagreement, not $s_i$.
The optional dense configuration adds a dense L1 term, a separately weighted
pooled-L1 term and a VICReg-style anti-collapse regulariser on $\hat z$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vr}}
=
\frac{1}{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\max\bigl(0,\,1-\mathrm{std}(\hat z_{\ldots,j})\bigr)
+
\frac{1}{D}\sum_{\substack{a,b=1\\a\neq b}}^{D}
\mathrm{Cov}(\hat z)_{ab}^{2}.
\label{eq:vicreg}
\end{equation}
Here all non-feature axes of $\hat z$ are flattened before computing standard
deviations and the $D{\times}D$ feature covariance, matching the implementation.
so that
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dense}}=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pool}}+
e^{-s_{\mathrm{L1d}}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{L1d}}+
s_{\mathrm{L1d}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{vr}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vr}}$.
STIR fine-tuning is evaluated as a separate encoder-adaptation experiment
rather than presented as part of the core pooled checkpoint.
Persistence (copy last latent) is a mandatory baseline for every forecast table.

\subsection{STIR point-track regulariser}
STIR provides IR start/end segmentations rather than dense tracks.
We sample up to $64$ points from each mask, map them onto the token grid, and apply a symmetric chamfer between tokens at those sites at the first and last timestep:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{STIR}}
=
\mathrm{Chamfer}\bigl(z_{0}[p_{\mathrm{start}}],\, z_{T'-1}[p_{\mathrm{end}}]\bigr).
\label{eq:stir}
\end{equation}
For feature sets $A$ and $B$, we use the symmetric mean
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{Chamfer}(A,B)
=
\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{a\in A}\min_{b\in B}\|a-b\|_2^2
+
\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{b\in B}\min_{a\in A}\|b-a\|_2^2 .
\end{equation*}
$p_{\mathrm{start}}$ and
$p_{\mathrm{end}}$ denote the corresponding sampled token-grid indices.
This optional loss adapts the unfrozen encoder in representation space, with
no pixel regression. The reported before/after STIR result comes from this
separate fine-tuning run; it is not evidence that the main full-H-JEPA
checkpoint was jointly trained with STIR.

\subsection{Physical alignment of latent actions}
SCARED stores a $4{\times}4$ camera pose per frame; C3VD provides \texttt{pose.txt}.
All matrices are normalised to camera-to-world convention; the relative pose
$\mathbf{T}_t^{-1}\mathbf{T}_{t+1}$ is mapped to a $6$D twist using one
SE(3) logarithm. Fractional tubelet centres use translation interpolation and
SO(3) interpolation before differencing; complete videos, not
transitions, are assigned to train, calibration and test splits.
The first (v1) physical cache encoded stacked stereo frames with a
bidirectional 64-frame encoder, which mixed both camera views and let latent
history carry future-frame content. The corrected v2 cache crops SCARED
rgb.mp4 to the single eye corresponding to the pose camera, encodes every
tubelet from a look-back window of eight tubelets (no future attention and no
chunk resets), and aligns poses at integer window-end frames. Model selection
for this branch uses leave-one-case-out cross-validation over the SCARED
training cases with a fixed hard-negative bank; the v1 test set is frozen as
an audit set (the frozen-test protocol file released with the paper). Samples are
$(z_{\mathrm{hist}},u_{t:t+H-1},z_{\mathrm{future}},d_{\mathrm{wall}})$,
where $d_{\mathrm{wall}}$ is the binary near-wall indicator from SCARED depth.
The legacy grounding diagnostic measures NMI and a residual-to-twist probe.
The corrected criterion is stronger: real actions must reduce future error
relative to within-batch shuffled actions, inverse-action $R^2$ must exceed
$0.3$, and at least $80\%$ of transitions must favour the real action.

\subsection{Factorised state and frozen-teacher fidelity}
A zero-initialised low-rank residual adapter maps each frozen teacher latent to
geometry $s^g$, tool $s^q$, slow semantic $s^m$ and nuisance $\xi$ slots. Only
$[s^g;s^q;s^m]$ is the planner-visible interface for the factorised-state
module (used by auxiliary losses and diagnostics; the implemented CEM/MPPI
planner itself rolls the pooled latent). A decoder reconstructs the detached
V-JEPA~2 teacher feature; cross-slot covariance is penalised. SCARED depth,
EndoVis masks and slow labels supervise their corresponding slots when
available. This prevents adaptation from discarding representation content
merely because it is difficult to forecast.

\subsection{Training procedure}
Clips are enumerated from a sequence-level manifest with video-level splits (a hash of the dataset and sequence identifier); no clip crosses a split boundary.
Sampling is domain-balanced by round-robin so laparoscopy does not dominate GI/bronchoscopy.
Dense tokens are cached once per encoder; L1, full H-JEPA, GRU, and Mamba train on the \emph{same} cached latents.
Optimisation: AdamW (lr $3{\times}10^{-4}$, weight decay $0.01$) with a fixed
learning rate and gradient clip $1.0$.
Full H-JEPA: $69.7$M trainable parameters; GRU: $2.63$M; Mamba: $1.58$M.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Audited Endo-HJEPA training and grounded planning (no pixel loss).}
\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Training:} for each domain-balanced clip $x_{1:T}$ with domain $d$:
\STATE \quad $z_{1:T'}\leftarrow f_\theta(x_{1:T})$
\STATE \quad $\mathcal{L}_1\leftarrow\mathrm{SmoothL1}(\mathrm{causal\text{-}L1}(z_{\mathrm{hist}},e_d),z_{\mathrm{fut}})$ \quad (residual)
\STATE \quad $\mathcal{L}_2\leftarrow\mathrm{SmoothL1}\!\bigl(\mathrm{L2}(\mathrm{pool}_2(z_{\mathrm{hist}}),e_d),\,z_{\mathrm{fut}}^{\,2,4,\ldots}\bigr)$
\STATE \quad if pose/depth is available: align fractional tubelet centres to interpolated SE(3) poses and compute $u_t=\log(\mathbf{T}_t^{-1}\mathbf{T}_{t+1})^\vee$
\STATE \quad factorise $z\to(s^g,s^q,s^m,\xi)$; preserve detached teacher feature
\STATE \quad train block-causal probabilistic dynamics, inverse action and near-wall risk
\STATE \textbf{Planning (offline):} optimise continuous $u_{t:t+H-1}$ on the pooled latent rollout by CEM/MPPI (the slot adapter feeds auxiliary losses only)
\STATE \quad reject high-risk/high-uncertainty candidates; otherwise execute first action in replay
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Why representations, not pixels}
\label{sec:theory}
Write a frame as $x=\rho(\mathsf{s},n)$ with idealised rendering map $\rho$:
structure $\mathsf{s}$ (lumen, pose,
instruments) evolves as
$\mathsf{s}_{t+1}=g(\mathsf{s}_t)+\varepsilon$ with
$\mathbb{E}\|\varepsilon\|_2^2=\sigma_s^2$, whereas appearance nuisance $n$
(highlights, fluid) retains conditional randomness after the observable
history $\mathcal H_t$. Define its irreducible image-space variance as
$\sigma_x^2=\mathbb{E}\|x_{t+1}-
\mathbb{E}[x_{t+1}\mid \mathsf{s}_{t+1},\mathcal H_t]\|_2^2$.
These are idealised quantities; $\mathsf{s}$ is distinct from the planner slots
$s^g,s^q,s^m$ and from the learnable log-variances $s_i$.

\textbf{Proposition 1 (pixel prediction is appearance-limited).}
Any next-frame predictor $\hat x_{t+1}=\hat r(x_{1:t})$ satisfies
$\mathbb{E}\|x_{t+1}-\hat x_{t+1}\|_2^2\ge\sigma_x^2$.
This is the conditional-variance decomposition of squared error: even an
oracle structural forecast cannot recover nuisance variability that remains
random given $\mathcal H_t$.

\textbf{Proposition 2 (representation prediction is structure-limited).}
If $\mathbb{E}[\rho(\mathsf{s},n)]=\phi(\mathsf{s})$ is appearance invariant and $\phi$ is
$L_\phi$-Lipschitz, then the oracle structural predictor
$\hat z_{t+1}=\phi(g(\mathsf{s}_t))$ satisfies
$\mathbb{E}\|z_{t+1}-\hat z_{t+1}\|_2^2
\le L_\phi^2\sigma_s^2$, with no additive $\sigma_x^2$ term.
(Here $\rho$ is the idealised render map, $\phi$ the structure-sufficient
encoder projection, and $\mathsf{s}$ the structure state defined above.)

These statements formalise the design choice; they do not by themselves prove downstream superiority.
That is tested in \S\ref{sec:rq1}, where a next-frame CNN (PSNR $17.9$\,dB) and a conditional DDPM ($4.9$\,dB) both lose to copy-last ($29.6$--$30.4$\,dB).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Goal.}
The experiments separate predictive validity from control validity: can
Endo-HJEPA forecast endoscopic representations better than persistence and
strong recurrent/SSM baselines; do emergent actions encode physical motion; and

video-level trial thresholds.

limits, not by dataset dump.

use the $2{,}000$-clip audited checkpoint; recognition uses the official
CholecT50 split. Discrete latent-MPC scores are excluded after the correctness
audit described in Section~\ref{sec:rq3}.
These protocols are labelled in every caption.

\paragraph{Visual corpus audit.}
Figure~\ref{fig:dataset-atlas} makes three properties hidden by aggregate frame
counts explicit. First, GI data span conventional colonoscopy, capsule imagery

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Latent forecast is cosine similarity and MSE between predicted and target \emph{pooled} tokens at horizon $h$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{cos}_h
=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}
\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
\frac{
\langle \hat z^{(v)}_{t+h},\, z^{(v)}_{t+h}\rangle
}{
\|\hat z^{(v)}_{t+h}\|_2\,\|z^{(v)}_{t+h}\|_2
},
\label{eq:cos}
\end{equation}
averaged over video-level val clips $\mathcal{V}$.
Higher cosine / lower MSE is better.
Physical planning requires at least 100 video-level trials; reach is the
fraction with lower terminal pose error than persistence. We additionally
report translation/rotation error, bootstrap confidence intervals, near-wall
AUC, Brier score, ECE and conformal coverage. The prespecified gates are
pose-delta $R^2>0.3$, real-action preference $>80\%$, near-wall AUC
$\ge0.75$ on at least 500 transitions, reach $\ge50\%$, and pose-error
reduction $\ge20\%$.
Recognition uses video-level linear-probe phase accuracy and instrument mAP (mean $\pm$ std over 3 seeds).
Action grounding uses NMI versus a random-id baseline.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Dynamics: persistence, GRU, Mamba/SSM, query-token L1.
Representation: ImageNet ViT, DINOv2, VideoMAE, TimeSformer, ViViT, all on the same official CholecT50 split.
Pixel contrast: next-frame CNN and conditional DDPM.
V-JEPA~2-AC and SurgRec-JEPA have no public endoscopic-planning checkpoints; we cite their papers and do not invent a numeric comparison.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Paired bootstrap (1{,}000 resamples) and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests against GRU and Mamba, Holm-corrected across horizons, on 750 video-level clips.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
Single GPU; encoder cached once; AdamW as in Section~\ref{sec:method}.
Hyperparameters: hidden $512$, $8$ heads, $4$ layers, dropout $0.1$,
$K{=}16$, history/horizon $4/4$. The optional dense path uses VICReg weight
$0.1$; headline pooled checkpoints do not activate it.
Varying depth ($2$--$6$), width ($256$--$768$) and lr ($10^{-4}$--$10^{-3}$) changes $6$k-clip cosine by ${<}0.002$.

\subsection{Capabilities 2 and 3: action-conditioned prediction and model-based navigation}
\label{sec:rq3}
A post-hoc audit found that the original L2/L3 predictor constructed, but
never executed, its Transformer encoder in the forward pass. Its outputs were
therefore not functions of history or candidate action. The earlier 98.0\% latent-reach and 92.8\% energy-ranking
values are retained only for provenance, marked invalid, and removed from all
claims. The corrected implementation executes block-causal attention; the
executable input-sensitivity tests of Section~\ref{sec:audit} verify history,
action and domain sensitivity, future-action
non-leakage, and gradients to the action projection and encoder.

On SCARED depth, energy flags near-wall transitions above chance (AUC $0.682$, Spearman $-0.471$, $n{=}132$; Figure~\ref{fig:aux}b).
This is a weak physical correlation, not a calibrated collision probability:
it misses the prespecified AUC $0.75$ and 500-transition gates.
A trained alternative does not close the gap: a three-member probabilistic
ensemble plus a calibrated near-wall head on real 5th-percentile scene depth
(3{,}832 test transitions, temperature and conformal calibration on the
validation split) reaches only AUC $0.54$; an input study selecting among
current-state, future-rollout and combined features on validation does not
help. A dense-token head with attention pooling over 256 spatial tokens fares
no better on held-out cases (test AUC $0.47$), where the near-wall base rate
shifts from 9.4\% (validation) to 43\% (test). Normalising labels per case
(causal running-quartile thresholds) removes the base-rate shift but not the
failure (test AUC $0.52$). Near-wall anticipation therefore remains an open
limitation, not a calibrated capability.

Goal-directed navigation to an \emph{arbitrary} SCARED viewpoint is a harder task: the legacy discrete branch reached $10\%$ of $10$ pilot trials (invalidated with the L3 audit).
With the corrected v2 dynamics and CEM in the model's \emph{normalised} action
space (candidates constrained to the behaviour support), offline
receding-horizon navigation on the frozen test case passes all prespecified
gates: 200 trials, reach $51.5\%$ (95\% CI $[44.5, 58.5]$), translation-error
reduction $29.0\%$ versus persistence, 100\% gate acceptance. This is a
model-based offline metric (reach means the planned rollout retrieves a pose
closer to the goal than persistence), not a robot execution claim.
Case-level retraining on 23 SCARED sequences (20,927 aligned RGB--pose frames)
and one external C3VD trajectory yields inverse-action $R^2=0.394$ and lower
mean test MSE for real than shuffled actions (0.326 vs 0.417, $n=2,130$).
However, real actions win on only 73.8\% of transitions, below the prespecified
80\% gate. The aggregate is uneven across datasets: evaluated per dataset
under the identical protocol, SCARED reaches 75.5\% (inverse $R^2=0.474$,
$n=1{,}999$) whereas the single held-out C3VD trajectory is near chance at
51.9\% (inverse $R^2=-8.7$, $n=131$). The external trajectory does not
generalise, and the pooled number overstates robustness. Because three model
variants were compared on this test set during the audit, we report the
best-scoring variant's metrics as an audit result rather than as confirmation
of a frozen protocol.

The audit traced the deficit to four correctable causes: stacked-stereo input
(two camera views squashed into one frame while the pose belongs to a single
camera), bidirectional 64-frame encoding (history latents could attend to
future frames), 64-frame chunk resets, and a train/evaluation mismatch in
counterfactual negatives (global shuffles in training versus local ones at
test time). The corrected v2 cache and protocol (single-eye crop, past-only
eight-tubelet look-back, window-end pose alignment, matched local hard
negatives and the fixed evaluation bank of Section~\ref{sec:audit},
leave-one-case-out development) lift the frozen-test real-action win fraction
from 73.8\% to \textbf{83.1\%} (fixed-bank 88.2\%, inverse $R^2=0.482$),
passing the 80\% gate; grouped CV over four SCARED cases gives 88.9\% macro
pair-win with a worst fold of 84.1\%, and three training seeds give
85.9--86.5\% fixed-bank pair-win (all gates passed). For C3VD, the
depth-reprojection gate of Section~\ref{sec:audit} with
the dataset's Scaramuzza intrinsics revealed that \texttt{pose.txt} requires an
OpenGL$\to$OpenCV axis flip in addition to the row/column transpose: the
transpose-only convention leaves 10.2\,px median reprojection error at a
five-frame gap with the official 0--100\,mm depth scale, versus 0.21\,px
after the flip. With corrected action labels,
the frozen v2 model's C3VD win fraction rises from 49.1\% (chance) to 63.6\%
($n=55$). Extending to fourteen additional C3VDv2 trajectories (ten with
usable windows after warm-up) gives above-chance action sensitivity on nine of
ten external trajectories (45.5--70.9\% per trajectory; pooled 58.3\%,
fixed-bank 66.6\%, inverse $R^2=0.338$, $n=798$). This is directional
generalisation, but still below the 80\% gate, so cross-dataset physical
generalisation remains an open limitation.
A slot-space ablation isolates the planner interface: factorised
geometry/tool/semantic states preserve action sensitivity (86.5\% fixed-bank
pair win) but collapse offline navigation (5.5\% reach versus 51.5\% pooled),
so MPC runs on the full pooled latent. The ensemble-risk and CEM/MPPI
implementations retain only synthetic verification pending a common SCARED
depth/risk evaluation.

\subsection{Limit A: cross-orifice transfer}
\label{sec:rq4}
A laparoscopy-only model beats persistence in-domain ($0.922$ vs $0.850$) but falls \emph{below} persistence on zero-shot GI ($0.791$ vs $0.913$) and bronchoscopy ($0.776$ vs $0.893$).
Fine-tuning only the domain embedding on $32$ target clips recovers $+0.045$
on GI (evaluation $n{=}10$) and $+0.049$ on bronchoscopy
(evaluation $n{=}9$; Figure~\ref{fig:planning}c), but both remain below
persistence. This is partial recovery, not successful zero-shot adaptation.
The unified model beats persistence in every domain (Table~\ref{tab:perdomain}).
Orifice domains are distinct enough that zero-shot transfer fails; unified training, or a cheap domain token, is necessary.

\subsection{Limit B: unsupervised latent actions are not grounded (negative result)}
\label{sec:rq5}
Emergent latent actions are not physically grounded: SCARED pose NMI is
$0.41$--$0.52$ versus random $0.49$--$0.53$, and exceeds its matched random
baseline on only two of six keyframes; residual$\to$twist $R^2$ is negative.
They are also not semantically grounded (CholecT50 verb NMI $0.044$ versus
random $0.056$; probe accuracy $0.306$ versus majority-class $0.390$).
A codebook-level verb loss does not help.
Encoder-level verb supervision lifts residual$\to$verb NMI from chance $0.026$ to $0.056$: above chance, still weak.
Latent actions index visual change; they are neither discovered surgical
actions nor admissible control variables.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Clinical significance}
The target is anticipatory endoscopic intelligence, not another phase-recognition point.
Each validated capability maps onto a concrete clinical pathway.
\emph{Loss-of-view warning}: Capability 1 forecasts how the view will evolve
before it is lost, the computational primitive behind collision and
loss-of-sight alerts.
\emph{Camera-handling training and assessment}: Capability 2 scores what a
measured camera motion would show, enabling objective evaluation of trainee
camera work without executing it.
\emph{Navigation assistance}: Capability 3 plans camera motion toward a target
view in the model, an algorithmic baseline for phantom or simulator
validation.
Unification is the data-efficient path for bronchoscopy, where labelled video is scarce.
All of this remains in-silico.
Clinical translation would require a pose/action-conditioned model, calibrated
time-to-contact or view-loss endpoints, prospective device-level testing, and
a human-factors study showing that warnings improve clinician performance
without increasing distraction. Latent cosine alone is not a clinical
endpoint.

\subsection{What the method actually contributes}
The validated innovations that move forecast numbers are residual anchoring,
causal L1 and domain tokens. The physical contribution is a
correctness-audited pipeline (continuous SE(3), normalised-action CEM,
calibration and hard rejection) that now passes its offline gates on SCARED,
while remaining model-based evidence rather than robot execution. This
distinction prevents a high latent similarity score from being misrepresented
as control.

\subsection{Failure analysis}
A small GRU is nearly competitive at $h{=}1$.
The original discrete action path failed an implementation sensitivity audit,
and unsupervised residual codes encode visual change, not steer commands;
only after metric SE(3) supervision, normalised-action CEM and the v2 data
corrections does offline navigation pass its gates. Calibrated near-wall risk
fails on real depth (AUC $0.54$), and C3VD external generalisation stays below
gate even after the pose-convention fix.
Zero-shot GI/bronch transfer fails because the domains are genuinely distinct at the dynamics level.
Each failure points to a concrete remedy (action supervision, domain-token adaptation) rather than to an overclaim.

\subsection{Comparison to published world models}
Endo-HJEPA occupies a distinct design point: hierarchical representation
prediction, unified across orifices, no pixel decoder, with physical control
claims gated by pose/depth evidence.
V-JEPA~2-AC and SurgRec release public checkpoints, but the former is
action-conditioned for a Franka robot and the latter is a recognition
encoder; neither is an endoscopic planner under our protocol. EndoWAM reports
physical phantom navigation without a public checkpoint, while SVWM evaluates
pixel generation. We therefore report their published task metrics in
Table~\ref{tab:sota-context} as non-comparable context rather than fabricate a
leaderboard.

\subsection{Limitations}
(i)~Training scale is far below SurgRec (${\sim}2{\times}10^8$ frames).
(ii)~Full Cholec80 depends on CAMMA access; further C3VD/C3VDv2 trajectories
remain Drive-quota-limited.
(iii)~ION has no public telemetry.
(iv)~Latent residual actions are not interpretable or controllable.
(v)~All planning is in-silico. The corrected continuous branch passes its
action-sensitivity gate on held-out SCARED cases but stays below the gate on
five external C3VD trajectories even after the pose-convention fix (pooled
57.9\%); ensemble risk and CEM/MPPI still have only synthetic verification.
(vi)~Val forecast is domain-balanced, so per-dataset $n$ is not proportional to corpus size; $n{=}1$ rows are omitted.
(vii)~Headline checkpoints use pooled latents; dense L1, VICReg,
specular/instrument weighting and STIR are optional extensions, not activated
together in those checkpoints.
(viii)~The original discrete-MPC values are invalid after the action-path
correctness audit and are excluded from evidence.
(ix)~One third of the 750-clip balanced forecast validation set comes from
private ION bronchoscopy, limiting independent reproduction; public-only and
external-site validation remain necessary.
(x)~The pooled forecast cache uses the encoder's bidirectional 16-frame
windows, so history tokens may carry limited future context within a window.
Matched audits at 2{,}000 and 6{,}000 clips with strictly past-only encoding
bound this inflation at $0.015$--$0.018$ cosine (0.9578 vs 0.9761 at 6k), and
the causal model still beats persistence (0.9102) under causal encoding. The
physical-action cache (v2) already uses past-only look-back encoding.

\section{Conclusion}
Endo-HJEPA brings hierarchical joint-embedding prediction to unified
cross-orifice endoscopic video and makes the boundary between prediction and
physical control explicit.
A causal residual forecaster surpasses persistence, GRU and Mamba on a
leakage-free protocol, and a per-dataset decomposition shows the forecast win
is not carried by a single corpus. Energy has a weak physical correlation, and
a trained calibrated risk head on real depth does not improve on it.
After the audit-driven corrections (single-eye input, past-only encoding,
local hard negatives, grouped-CV development), the continuous SE(3) path
passes its 80\% action-sensitivity gate on held-out SCARED cases (83.1\%),
and behaviour-constrained continuous CEM passes the offline navigation gates
(51.5\% reach, 200 trials).
A reprojection-gated pose fix plus four additional C3VDv2 trajectories give
above-chance action sensitivity on all five external trajectories (pooled
57.9\%), still below gate. Risk calibration and robot-executed planning
therefore remain open, not navigation claims.
We release the protocol, the verified metric file, and the figure generators so that every number in this paper can be regenerated from the released result files.
